## 10.1 总纲：方法地图 ≠ 执行能力

UMoD 用一页篇幅讲清"田野访问是什么"，Goodman 用一整章讲"怎么去"：

> **原文（DOC 16 · Ch.9 "Field Visits" §43）**：
> 「Since you'll be going **out of your office and into the workplaces and homes of your customers**, it's especially important to be **thoroughly prepared**. You won't have the option to **go back and get something you've forgotten**, and **you—not your company's cool new leather waiting room furniture—will be the one making the first impression** about your company.」

> **★★★★ 判据：田野访问的核心困难是"不可返回"——漏了东西就是漏了。**
> 这与 UMoD 的"方法选择"层面完全是两件事：
> **UMoD 回答"去不去看"，Goodman 回答"去了别丢人、别漏东西"。**

## 10.2 选人：两种策略，取决于项目目标

> **原文（DOC 16 · Ch.9 §53）**：
> 「There are **two main strategies** for selecting sites and people for observation. Your choice of strategy will **reflect the priority of your project**. **If your goal is to develop a wide range of new opportunities for design**, you may want to follow the **extreme or lead user** strategy. **If your goal is to solve a specific problem**, you may want to follow the **typical user** strategy.」

| 策略 | 适用目标 | 选谁 | 原文例子（莲蓬头项目） |
|---|---|---|---|
| **典型用户（typical user）** | **解决具体问题** | 关键因素上最主流的人 | — |
| **极端/领先用户（extreme/lead user）** | **开拓新的设计机会** | 某方面极端的人：极热爱、极抵触、或"领先用户" | 找"特别爱洗澡、为完美淋浴投入很多"的人，对比"讨厌洗澡、只泡澡"的人 |

> **原文（DOC 16 · Ch.9 §58）**：
> 「You may find that **extreme users make visible behaviors or desires that are present in users that are more "typical" but are harder to see**. You may also find that widely divergent participants may **suggest a broader set of opportunities** than a narrow（though deep）study of one particular group.」

> **★★★★ 判据：极端用户的价值是"把典型用户身上隐形的需求放大到可见"。**
> 这是"为什么要看极端案例"的最好解释——**不是极端案例本身重要，是它把常态照出来了**。

> **原文（DOC 16 · Ch.9 §55，典型用户策略）**：
> 「After specifying your target audience, identify the **most important activities and groups of customers**… **Focus your research on participants who share the most common key factors until you feel that you know what there is to know about their behavior**, and then **move on to secondary markets**.」

> **★★★ 判据：典型用户策略的执行顺序是"先主力、后次要"，且切换点由"你觉得自己已经知道了"决定。**
> 这是一个主观饱和判据——**原文没给量化标准**。

### 10.2.1 目标人群怎么描述（五问，可直接当筛选表）

> **原文（DOC 16 · Ch.9 §46–§51）**：
> 「You should specify this target audience in **as much detail as you can, concentrating on their behavior**.
> • What is their **demographic makeup**?
> • What **activities** are most significant to their relationship with the product?
> • What **tools**（digital and otherwise）do they regularly use in those activities?
> • Are there **tools they must occasionally use** to solve specific problems?
> • **How do they use them**?」

> **★★★ 判据：五问里四问是行为与工具，只有一问是人口统计。**
> 原文的定性词是「concentrating on their **behavior**」——**人口统计只是定位工具，不是研究目标**。
> 这与 ch08 §8.2.1「人口统计不是行为」同源。

> **原文（DOC 16 · Ch.9 §52）**：
> 「Take this list and **narrow it down to a few key factors**. They may be demographic, such as **location and income**, or behavioral, such as **tools used and attitudes** toward the product/activity.」

> **★★ 判据："几个关键因素"是后续定样本量的乘数——所以要先收敛到少数几个。**
> 因为样本量公式是"每因素 4~8 人"（见下），因素越多，总量越大。

### 10.2.2 人数（与 ch06 呼应）

> **原文（DOC 16 · Ch.9 §61）**：
> 「We think designer **Kim Goodwin's suggestion of four people per important factor**… is a good starting point **for a quick project**… you might interview **four people who shower a lot and four people who don't like to shower at all**.」

> **原文（DOC 16 · Ch.9 §62）**：
> 「**If you have more time, five to eight people per factor** should give you a pretty good idea of how a big chunk of the target audience does their work… and should be **enough for a first round of inquiry**. **If you find that you have not met the goals of the research or you don't feel comfortable with the results of the first round, schedule a second round**.」

| 时间条件 | 每因素人数 | 原文定位 |
|---|---|---|
| **快速项目** | **4 人** | 「a good starting point for a **quick** project」 |
| **时间充裕** | **5~8 人** | 「enough for a **first round** of inquiry」 |
| 不满意时 | **加第二轮** | 「schedule a second round」 |

> **★★★★ 判据：4 人与 5~8 人不是矛盾，是"快速版"与"标准版"两档。**
> 原文用「If you have more time」明确把二者条件化了。
> **这是本技能里唯一一组"给出两档并说明取舍条件"的样本量建议。**

> **★★★ 判据：第一轮不满意就"加第二轮"，而不是"第一轮多约人"。**
> 原文的处置是迭代的：**先做一轮看结果，不够再来一轮**。

### 10.2.3 招募问卷的写法

> **原文（DOC 13 · Ch.6 §105）**：
> 「• **Prioritize helpful participants.** There is nothing more frustrating than an interview with someone who **hates talking** or a usability test with someone who **won't follow directions**. **Including open-ended questions can help the recruiter guess whether a person will be responsive and articulate.**」

> **★★★ 判据：招募问卷里的开放题不只是筛条件，是在筛"这人愿不愿意说话/听不听话"。**
> 成本极低（多加一两道开放题），收益是整场访谈的质量。

> **原文（DOC 13 · Ch.6 §117 / §127，筛选条件实例）**：
> 「• Have created **at least five eCalendar events** in last month」
> 「• Has **at least five scheduled appointments per week**」

> **★★★★ 判据：合格线要写成"能对着事实核对的数量"，不是"你觉得他像用户"。**
> 「每月至少建 5 个日程事件」「每周至少 5 个预约」——这类条件招募方**可以逐条核实**。

## 10.3 约人：排期与预期管理

> **原文（DOC 16 · Ch.9 §64）**：
> 「Observational research sessions can last from **a couple of hours to multiple full workdays**, depending on the length of the tasks and how much ancillary information you'll be collecting. **The most important criterion in scheduling is that the people need to be doing the kinds of activity you're going to study while you're observing them.** You may need to **negotiate timing in order to show up when they're doing the relevant tasks**. You may need to ask them to wait until you arrive. If you are doing an **on-site interview**（rather than a full observation activity）, it may only take you **an hour or two**.」

| 场次类型 | 时长（原文） |
|---|---|
| 完整观察 | **几小时 ~ 连续数个工作日** |
| **现场访谈**（非完整观察） | **1~2 小时** |

> **★★★★ 判据：排期的第一原则是"我到的那个时段，他确实在做我要看的事"——不是"双方都方便"。**
> 原文用「**The most important criterion**」定位这条。
> **实务含义：宁可约在奇怪的时间，也不能约在他在做别的事的时间。**

> **原文（DOC 16 · Ch.9 §65）**：
> 「If you are interested in **complex activities achieved by multiple people working together**, it is a good idea to schedule **multiple days of visits, with a team of more than two people**… During the day, you should also **make time at least once to meet with other people on your team to share what you're seeing** and discuss any emerging insights into patterns of behavior.」

> **★★★ 判据：研究协作型复杂活动时，配置要求是"多天 + 两人以上团队 + 每天至少一次队内碰头"。**
> 因为你要看的是"工作如何咬合"，单人单日看不出来。

> **原文（DOC 16 · Ch.9 §66）**：
> 「If you do not have the **time, budget, or team members** to make all the visits you think are necessary, it's time to **revisit your research plan** and **rethink how you will answer your research questions**—or if you need to **rescope your goals** for the project.」

> **★★★★ 判据：资源不够时的正确动作是"改研究问题"，不是"少去几家凑合"。**
> 原文给的是重新规划（revisit the plan / rescope the goals），不是"勉强做完"。

### 10.3.1 到场前的告知（一条很实用的清单）

> **原文（DOC 16 · Ch.9 §67）**：
> 「Since the research is going to be on-site, give the participants some idea of what to expect when you arrive. **Before you show up, tell them**: the **general goals** of the research, **how long it will take**, the **equipment you will use**, and **what kinds of activities you want to observe**. You don't have to be specific（and, in fact, **leaving some specifics out can produce a more spontaneous response**）, but they should have a good idea of what you are asking of them…
> You will need to **get consent from all participants before taking photographs or video** of them…
> Finally, **ask them not to prepare for your arrival at all**. People tend to **tidy up** their living and working space when a stranger arrives, so make it clear that it's important for you to see their daily environment, **warts and all**.」

**告知四件事**：

| # | 要提前告知 |
|---|---|
| 1 | 研究的大致目标 |
| 2 | 要占用多长时间 |
| 3 | 会用什么设备 |
| 4 | 想看哪类活动 |

> **★★★★ 判据：「请他们不要为你的到来做任何准备」是一条必须主动说出口的要求。**
> 原文给了理由：人会整理空间（tidy up），而你要看的正是"warts and all"（连瑕疵一起）。
> **不说这句话，你看到的是一间被打扫过的屋子。**

> **★★★ 判据：告知"不必太具体"，因为「leaving some specifics out can produce a more spontaneous response」。**
> 这是"告知目标但不预告细节"的分寸——**知道要发生什么，但不知道具体会问什么。**

## 10.4 准备：一份可以直接照做的清单

> **原文（DOC 16 · Ch.9 §84–§89）**：
> 「• Make a **list of everything you're going to bring**—every pencil, consent form, and notebook. **Start the list a week before** you're going to visit your first site… **A day before** you visit the site, make sure you have everything on the list… **On the day** of the visit, cross off everything as it's loaded into your backpack or car.…
> • Make sure you have **twice as many media releases and/or consent forms** as you expect to need… more people could show up than you planned for.…
> • Put everything you need to make and track **incentive payments in one container**…
> • **Know how to operate your equipment.** Set up a test site that will simulate the user's work environment ahead of time. **A day or two before, set up everything as you're going to use it on-site**… Then, **break it down and set it back up again**. Get a set of **good headphones to check the quality of the audio**…
> • **Have more than enough supplies.** Bring an **extension cord with multiple outlets**, extra recording media, two extra pads of paper, and a couple of extra pens… Take **extra batteries**…
> • **Plan for meal breaks and debrief time.** Closely watching someone for several hours can be **draining**… Bring bottled water and plan to eat between sessions.」

| # | 准备项 | 原文要求 | 时间点 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **物品清单** | 逐件列出（铅笔、同意书、笔记本） | **首访前一周开始**，前一天核对，当天逐件划掉 |
| 2 | **同意书** | 按预期的**两倍**准备 | 前一天 |
| 3 | **报酬** | 装在一个可低调携带的容器里 | 当天 |
| 4 | **设备演练** | **完整装一遍→拆掉→再装一遍** | 一两天前 |
| 5 | **耗材余量** | 多孔插排、备用存储、多两本便签、多几支笔、**备用电池** | 当天 |
| 6 | **体力** | 准备饮水、安排饭点与复盘时间 | 当天 |

> **★★★★ 判据：设备要"装一遍、拆掉、再装一遍"——只装一次不算准备。**
> 原文把这一步写成了明确动作。理由不用多解释：**现场没有第二次机会**（§43）。

> **★★★ 判据：同意书按"两倍"准备——因为「more people could show up than you planned for」。**
> 漏了同意书 = 那天的观察内容不能对外使用。

> **★ 一个可复用的准备细节**：「good headphones to check the quality of the audio」——
> 原文判断「Good audio quality can make the difference between **usable and useless** media files」。

## 10.5 到场：六种必须避免的角色

这是 Goodman 全章最独特、UMoD 完全没有的内容。

> **原文（DOC 16 · Ch.9 §103）**：
> 「There are also **several relationships to avoid**.」

| # | 要避免的角色 | 原文描述 | 后果 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **采访者/被采访者** | 「The interviewer/interviewee… Interviewees won't reveal details unless specifically asked. **You want the participant's work and thoughts to drive the interview.**」 | 变成记者提问，用户被动应答 |
| 2 | **专家/新手** | 「Although you may be the expert in design, **the participants are experts in their own domain**.」 | 用户反过来问你怎么办，观察中断 |
| 3 | **客服/投诉部** | 「you are **not the complaint department**… giving too much time to them can turn your observation session into **tech support**.」 | 变成售后，且可能得罪销售 |
| 4 | **客人** | 「**Don't be a guest**… You are there to understand how they do their work, not to enjoy their hospitality.」 | 寒暄占掉全场 |
| 5 | **老大哥** | 「**big brother**… You are **not there to evaluate or criticize** the performance of the people who you are observing.」 | 被观察者表演合规流程 |
| 6 | （隐含）**间谍** | 「Reporting on people's actions and opinions **without their knowledge and explicit consent is always unethical**.」 | 伦理与法律风险 |

> **★★★★ 判据（角色 1）：一旦你开始"提问驱动"，访谈就夺走了工作本身的驱动权。**
> 原文给的纠正动作很具体：「When you find yourself acting as a journalist, prompting the participant before
> he or she says something, **take a deep breath and give the person you're watching time to act or talk**.」
> **判据化：沉默是你的工具，不是冷场。**

> **★★★★ 判据（角色 2）：必须一开始就说清"我不是来帮你解决问题的"。**
> 原文引 Beyer & Holtzblatt：「Set the customer's expectation correctly at the beginning by explaining that
> **you are there to hear about and see their work because only they know their work practice. You aren't
> there to help them with problems or answer questions.**」
> 且给了应对追问的技巧：**用非指导性访谈把问题反回去**——「How would you expect it to act?」

> **★★★ 判据（角色 5/6）：被当成"上面派来查绩效的"，整场数据就废了。**
> 原文给的预防动作：「get permission from management to **contact and schedule people yourself** rather than
> having the request come as a demand from above」。
> **即：让你去约人，别让管理层派单。**

> **★★★★ 判据（伦理红线）：向管理层汇报某个员工的言行而当事人不知情，"always unethical"。**
> 原文还说这可能**违反劳动法**、并违背你对被访者的保密承诺。
> 处置：「clearly explain the ethical and legal problems with this request to management」。

### 10.5.1 应当建立的关系：师徒 vs 伙伴

> **原文（DOC 16 · Ch.9 §99–§101）**：
> 「**observational research (or what they call "contexual inquiry")** tends to follow **two main patterns**:
> 1. **The master/apprentice model** introduces you as the **apprentice** and the person you're observing as the **master**. You learn his or her craft by watching. Occasionally, the apprentice can ask a question or the master can explain a key point, but **the master's primary role is to do his or her job, narrating what he or she is doing while doing it**（without having to think about it or explain why）. This keeps the "master craftsman" focused on details, **avoiding the generalizations**.
> 2. **Partnership** extends the master/apprentice model. The interviewer **partners** with the participant in discovering the details of work. The partner **asks questions in order to surface problems and ways of working**. The participant is occasionally invited to **step back and consider the reasons for his or her behavior**. This discussion can make the participant **aware of the elements of his or her work that are normally invisible** to him or her.」

> **⚠ 原文拼写说明**：上引 `contexual inquiry` 是**原书 EPUB 文本的拼写**（`contextual` 少一个 `t`）。
> 已核对原始文本字节：`call “contexual inquiry”）`。**本版照录不改**，以保持引文可核对。
> 本文档正文其他位置一律用标准拼写 `contextual inquiry`——两者并存不是错字。

| 模式 | 你的角色 | 对方做什么 | 强项 | 代价 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **师徒（master/apprentice）** | 徒弟 | **做本职 + 边做边讲**（不必解释为什么） | **压住"概括化"**，保住细节 | 拿不到"为什么" |
| **伙伴（partnership）** | 伙伴 | 被邀请**退一步想自己的理由** | 让"平时看不见的部分"显形 | 会改变行为 |

> **★★★★ 判据：师徒模式的独特价值是「avoiding the generalizations」——它专治"用户只会讲总结版"。**
> 这与 UMoD p48「most people will summarize their work activities and speak abstractly」完全咬合：
> **UMoD 诊断了病症，Goodman 给了药方（让师傅边做边讲，不必解释为什么）。**

> **★★★ 判据：伙伴模式会改变行为，但原文说"值"——「Although this can potentially alter the participant's behavior, it can also provide critical information.」**
> 这是本技能里少见的"接受偏差换取信息"的明确取舍。

> **★★ 判据：两种模式都可以问"显得愚蠢的问题"。**
> 原文：「feel free to ask questions that seem **obvious, stupid, or naïve**. Those are the kinds of questions that can help you **explode your own assumptions**.」

### 10.5.2 建立关系（rapport）的定义

> **原文（DOC 16 · Ch.9 §98）**：
> 「one of the most important activities in observation is **establishing a rapport** with them. "Rapport" here means **a comfortable working relationship**. It **doesn't require friendship**, but it does mean that people should **not be afraid of you or hostile to your presence**.」

> **★★★ 判据：rapport 不是"交朋友"，是"对方不怕你、不敌视你"——下限而非上限。**
> 这个定义很实用：**它把"我跟他聊得挺好"从主观感受变成了可检验的状态**。

## 10.6 预期管理与反身性（本技能最好的一段方法论）

> **原文（DOC 16 · Ch.9 §78）**：
> 「As part of your preparation, **get clear about your expectations**. **Write down how and when you expect people to do things** that are important to your product, and **what attitudes you expect they will have**. You can do this with other members of the development team… **When you're in the field, keep these scenarios in mind. Use the situations where what you see doesn't match your expectations to trigger more investigation.**」

> **★★★★ 判据：把你的预期"写下来"，然后专门盯"哪里不符"——这是可执行的偏差管理。**
> 注意因果：**写预期不是为了验证，是为了知道该往哪深挖**（trigger more investigation）。
> 这与 ch03 §3.1.2「find what you are looking for」的警告正好互补：
> **不写预期，你会无意识地找证据；写了预期，你才有对照物。**

> **原文（DOC 16 · Ch.9 §79）**：
> 「Some people will tell you to take a "**neutral**" or "**unbiased**" position to field visits. However, we think **total objectivity is an unrealistic expectation**… Instead of futilely trying to rid yourself of personal beliefs and preferences, we recommend **reflexivity** instead. **Be clear to yourself and others about your expectations for the project, then work actively to counteract them.** That way, you can enter each new situation with a **more open mind** to its possibilities.」

> **★★★★ 判据：「完全客观是不切实际的预期」——审稿式的替代方案是"反身性"（reflexivity）。**
> 反身性的三个动作：**说清自己的预期 → 主动对抗它 → 保持开放**。
> **这条直接否定了"研究者应该清空自己"的常见说法**（也回应了 UMoD p61「open mind, not empty head」）。

> **原文（DOC 16 · Ch.9 §80–§81）**：
> 「Sometimes real-life situations unfold **very differently** from your expectations. You may be counting on one kind of situation… and you find something completely different, such as a **crisis** where the main system is down…
> In such situations, **pay attention to how the unexpected situation is resolved and compare that to the situation you had expected**… If the situation is very atypical… **try to get them to describe their normal routine**, maybe in contrast to what they're doing at the moment. If the situation seems like it's **too far off** from what you're trying to accomplish, **reschedule the visit**.」

| 现场情况 | 处置（原文） |
|---|---|
| **偏了但可救** | 追问"平时是怎样的"作为对照 |
| **偏得太远** | **改期**（reschedule） |
| 出意外（系统宕机等） | 看它**怎么被解决**，并与预期对照 |

> **★★★ 判据：「偏了」本身是数据——如危机如何被解决；但「偏得太远」就别硬做。**
> 原文给了明确的二分处置，不是"尽量采集"。

## 10.7 视频的三重风险（UMoD 未覆盖）

> **原文（DOC 16 · Ch.9 §93–§96）**：
> 「• Video **can give the impression that it is an objective and complete recording** of what happens. **That's not true!** Video captures just **one perspective**—the perspective of the person who shot it. As one remedy, architectural researcher Jonathan Bean suggests **handing a second camera over to a participant** so that the participant can document his or her own perspective.
> • **Managing the camera…can actually prevent you from seeing what you came to see.** Don't get so focused on working with the camera that you can't **ask questions and make decisions on the fly**. Having **one team member act as videographer and another focus on asking questions** can help solve this problem.
> • Video may **not always be appropriate** everywhere, especially where **privacy** is important or **informed consent may be impossible**… in a hospital, you may need to develop alternatives to video—and **get good at taking fast, detailed notes**.
> • **Video takes a long time to analyze.** Many people put lots of energy into taking hours of video, only to realize that **they have no time to watch it later**. Be realistic about whether you'll ever watch that footage again before you invest a lot of time in taking and processing it.」

| # | 风险 | 原文对策 |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **伪客观性**：只有一个视角 | **把第二台相机交给被试**，让他记录自己的视角 |
| 2 | **操作相机反而不看** | **一人掌机、一人提问**（分工） |
| 3 | **隐私/同意的场合不宜** | 改用**快速详细笔记** |
| 4 | **分析太费时** | **先问自己"我真的会再看吗"** |

> **★★★★ 判据（风险 1）：视频不是"客观记录"，是"拍摄者视角的记录"。**
> 这条判据对设计研究很重要，因为团队常把录像当成"事实本身"。
> **补救办法（交给被试一台相机）落地成本极低，值得默认采用。**

> **★★★★ 判据（风险 4）：拍之前先问"我会不会再看"——这是对录像冲动最有效的一句拦截。**
> 原文观察到很普遍的现象：「hours of video…no time to watch it later」。

## 10.8 数据与伦理的具体约束

### 10.8.1 报酬

> **原文（DOC 16 · Ch.9 §69）**：
> 「The **incentive payment** to each participant should **reflect the length of the observation**. Generally, this means **something between $100 and $200 for most visits**. Some companies may have **policies restricting such payments to their employees**, which should be determined **ahead of time**…」

> **★★ 判据：原文给了具体金额区间（$100–$200），但绑定"多数到访场景"，且要先查公司政策。**
> 另需注意：**研究被访者所在公司的员工时，可能有禁止付费的政策。**

### 10.8.2 保密与保密协议

> **原文（DOC 16 · Ch.9 §68）**：
> 「When studying people in **office environments**, it's often necessary to get **releases** and to sign **nondisclosure agreements**. Sometimes it's possible to do **stealth research** under the promise of anonymity, but **this approach brings real ethical and pragmatic problems**. **When you do not tell people you are a researcher, you are essentially spying on them. You are violating their privacy.** Pragmatically, field visits are hard to overlook…」

> **★★★★ 判据：「不告诉对方你是研究者」= 偷窥（spying），且实践上也难以隐藏。**
> 这条与 UMoD p159「shadowing is not intended to be a covert research method」同调，
> 但 Goodman 说得更直白，并补了实践层面的论据（相机、笔记本藏不住）。

### 10.8.3 匿名幻象

> **原文（DOC 13 · Ch.6 §234）**：
> 「**Preconceptions about your company** can also bias the attitudes of people coming to test with you. A **famous name can be intimidating**, and an **unknown company or product can seem more trivial** than it really is.」

> **★★★ 判据：公司名气本身是偏差源——知名让人拘谨，无名让人轻视。**
> 这条常被忽略：**招募话术里怎么介绍自己，会影响数据。**

## 10.9 分析：一句话定义了什么不是分析

> **原文（DOC 22 · Ch.15 "Analyzing Qualitative Data" §141，引 Kim Goodwin）**：
> 「If a piece of data no longer fits in a group, **move it somewhere more suitable**. If the group itself no longer seems justified, **break the group apart and redistribute the pieces** into existing or new groups. Continue until you feel confident that your clusters show **identifiable patterns**, but **don't get too obsessed about perfecting them**. As Kim Goodwin writes in *Designing for the Digital Age*, "**The diagram itself is not the point of the exercise; insight is**".」

> **★★★★ 判据：「图本身不是目的，洞见才是」——这是分析阶段最容易失守的地方。**
> 原文同时给了两条动作（搬条目、拆组）和一条警告（别追求完美）。
> **判据化：如果你在纠结便签摆得对不对，说明你已经在做美工而非分析。**

> **★ 组内条目的处置原则**：不合群的**移走**；组本身不成立的**拆掉重分**。
> 这两条是亲和图的日常维护规则，ch07 讲了结构，本章补了"迭代怎么维护"。

### 10.9.1 测试后的即时复盘

> **原文（DOC 18 · Ch.11 §304）**：
> 「• **Take notes immediately after**, writing down all interesting behaviors, errors, likes, and dislikes. **Discuss the test with any observers for 10–20 minutes immediately after** and take notes on their observations, too.」

> **★★★ 判据：测试后 10~20 分钟必须与观察者复盘并各自记笔记——记忆会很快衰减。**
> 注意顺序：**先各自记，再讨论**（避免互相污染）。
