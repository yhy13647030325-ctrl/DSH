## 7.1 总纲：测中要控制的三件事

测中有三件事同时发生：**被试在做事｜主持人在采集｜观察者在看**。本章处理的是"说与不说"的边界——因为**主持人的话是被试唯一能收到的污染源**。

> **原文（p193）**：
> 「Running a successful usability test requires **observers to adhere to strict guidelines so participants feel comfortable and willing to share information.**」

## 7.2 观察者准则（原文图 8-1，p193 全文照录要点）

> **原文（p193）**：
> 「To make sure your presence as an observer does not cause discomfort to participants or otherwise affect the quality of the data we collect, please observe the following rules:」

| # | 规则（原文） | 原文理由 |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 「**Arrive before the Session Is Scheduled to Start.**」 | 「Participants may interpret your lateness as **disregard for what they have to say**, and a stranger entering the room is distracting and disruptive.」 |
| 2 | 「**You Must Stay for the Duration.** ... If you cannot stay for the duration of the session, **please do not come at all**.」 | 「Having people constantly coming in and going out is very distracting.」 |
| 3 | 「**Turn off Your cell Phone or Leave It at Your Desk.** If you bring your computer, turn off email and instant messaging.」 | 「It's a short session. Please be fully present and ready to pay attention to the moderator and the participant.」 |
| 4 | 「**No Laughing, Grunting, Aha-ing, or Distracting Body Language.**」 | 「Participants may think you are laughing at them. ... It is important that observers do not make facial expressions or utter comments during the session.」 |
| 5 | 「**Ask Open-Ended Questions about what happened in the session** when invited by the moderator to do so. **Don't offer design or feature alternatives. Avoid asking about preferences or opinions.**」 | — |
| 6 | 「**Keep the Participant's Identity Confidential.** We have promised the participants that their identity will be kept confidential.」 | — |

> **★★★★ 判据：观察者规则里最容易被违反的是第 4 条（不笑、不出声、不做表情）**
> 原文给的机制很具体：「Participants may think you are **laughing at them**」。笑一声就够毁掉一个被试的坦率度。
> **落地**：把这张表在开场前发给观察者并口头过一遍（原文 p192：「Consider conducting a **short briefing meeting** before you start conducting sessions」）。

> **原文（p192）**：
> 「**Figure 8-1 assumes that observers will be in the room with the participant, but most of the rules apply even if the observers watch from a separate room, especially if the room is not sound proof.**」

> **★★ 判据：隔间不是免死金牌——不隔音时规则照旧**
> 实操：先测隔音，再决定要不要放宽。

## 7.3 开场脚本（Orientation Script）

### 7.3.1 脚本的三个作用

> **原文（p193）**：
> 「The orientation script (also known as the introduction script) is a communications tool meant to be **read verbatim** to participants. It describes what will happen during a test session, **sets the tone** for that session in the minds of the participants, and is intended to **put them at ease**.」

> **原文（p193）**：
> 「It achieves this by informing the participants of what they will be doing, and **reinforcing the fact that the product, not the participant, is being tested**.」

> **原文（p193）**：
> 「For a particularly nervous participant, introducing the session this way can provide **reassurance that he or she is actually the "right" person in the "right" place**.」

### 7.3.2 三条写作准则

| # | 准则（原文 p194） | 原文要点 |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 「Keep the Tone of the Script **Professional, but Friendly**」 | 「It should not be chummy or overly familiar as if the participants were your buddies.」 |
| 2 | 「Keep the Speech **Short**」 | 「limit the orientation script to **a few paragraphs**. Anything longer than that will not be retained」 |
| 3 | 「Plan to Read the Script to Each Participant **Verbatim**」 | 见 §7.3.3 |

> **原文（p194）**：
> 「Any instructions beyond **two pages** probably means that you are including actual test materials, such as task scenarios, as part of your script.」

> **★★ 判据：脚本不超过"N 段 + 两页"**
> 两页是硬上限；正常应"几段"。

### 7.3.3 为什么必须逐字念

> **原文（p195）**：
> 「**Do not attempt to memorize the script, paraphrase it, or simply "wing it" from session to session.**」

五条理由（原文 p195–p196）：

| # | 理由（原文） |
|---|---|
| 1 | 「You want to present the **exact same information** to each participant so that all the participants are exposed to **identical conditions** prior to the test.」 |
| 2 | 「People are easily influenced by past events.」——原文举例：前四个被试三个表现很差，你会不自觉把挫败感投射到第五个；「**reading the script forces you to use the same language**, which in turn makes it easier to control the nonverbal aspects of your communication」 |
| 3 | 「**More than one test moderator** may be conducting the test over a period of time ... there is a need to minimize the differences」 |
| 4 | 「Those members of the development team not present at the sessions will want to know **precisely what was said**」 |
| 5 | 「**You may forget an important point.**」 |

> **原文（p196）**：
> 「If you feel self-conscious reading the script or feel that it sets an overly formal tone, **simply tell the participant why you are reading it.** For example, "I'm going to read this script to you now so that I provide the exact instructions to you that I provide to everybody else, and so that I do not forget anything of importance."」

> **★★★★ 判据：逐字念不是为了规范，是为了"抗情绪污染"**
> 第 2 条理由是全章最深刻的：**主持人的情绪会通过措辞泄漏**，而念稿把"措辞"这一层锁死了。
> 实操含义：**连"不好意思我念稿"这句解释都应该写进脚本**。

### 7.3.4 脚本的十个内容块（原文 p197–p199）

| # | 块（原文小标题） | 关键要求（原文） |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 「Make Introductions」 | 「**Never volunteer the fact that any person associated with the test or observing in the same room has worked on the product**」 |
| 2 | 「Offer Refreshments」 | 「They are more apt to indulge if you already have a cup of coffee, soda, or water in your hand.」 |
| 3 | 「Explain Why the Participant Is Here」 | 「Do not feel you need to provide product history, number of participants being tested」 |
| 4 | 「Describe the Testing Setup」 | 指向设备、说明是否有人在镜后看、是否录像 |
| 5 | 「Explain What Is Expected of the Participant」 | 「**Encourage them to perform as they normally would**」 |
| 6 | 「Assure the Participant That He or She Is Not Being Tested」 | 见 §7.3.5 |
| 7 | 「Explain Any Unusual Requirements」 | 特殊设备的操作要先示范 |
| 8 | 「Mention That It Is Okay to Ask Questions at Any Time」 | 但要说明你**可能不回答** |
| 9 | 「Ask for Any Questions」 | 「ask them to **parrot back** a particular point」 |
| 10 | 「Refer to Any Forms That Need to Be Completed」 | 背景问卷、知情同意、录像许可 |

> **原文（p197）**：
> 「**Never volunteer the fact that any person associated with the test or observing in the same room has worked on the product** that the participants will be testing. Of course, if a participant asks (hardly anyone ever does), then by all means tell the participant the truth, but do not volunteer that information.」

> **原文（p197）**：
> 「The reason for this is simple; **you want the participants to feel absolutely unencumbered about providing any negative feedback.**」

> **★★★★ 判据："不主动说、问就说真话"——这是可执行的诚实边界**
> 不是撒谎（问就答），是**不主动提供会抑制负面反馈的信息**。
> 与 §7.5.2 的"不得对被试撒谎关于录像/观察"形成对照：**关于产品归属可以不说，关于被试自身处境必须说清。**

> **原文（p198）**：
> 「Let them know if:」
> - 「People are watching from behind a one-way mirror or from another room via cameras. **Do not get cute here** and say "Oh that old thing. It's just so we can see all sides of the equipment."」
> - 「The session is being recorded. **It is never a good idea to lie to participants about being observed or recorded in order not to make them nervous. First of all, it is not ethical.**」

> **原文（p197–p198，跨页）**：
> 「In no way, shape, or form」（p197 末）
> 「should the performance of the participants be tied to their compensation for participating, even as a joke. You can be sure that the thought has crossed their mind. No need to reinforce it.」（p198 初）

> **★★★ 判据："按表现给钱"是绝对禁区，玩笑也不行**
> 原文理由：被试本来就担心这件事，**提一句就坐实**。

### 7.3.5 "不是在测你"这句话的正确用法

> **原文（p199）**：
> 「This is probably the most familiar adage of testing, and **you should certainly say this**. However, **do not hold out hope that they will necessarily believe you just because you say it.** This slogan has become the "**it's for your own good**" slogan of our youth. **It is often repeated but never believed.**」

> **原文（p199）**：
> 「**Only the manner in which the test is conducted, the way in which you react to the person's behavior, and the behavior of the observers** will cause the truth of this adage to sink in.」

> **★★★★ 判据："不是在测你"必须说，但说的作用接近于零——靠行为兑现**
> 三个兑现渠道：① 测试怎么进行 ② **你怎么回应被试的行为** ③ 观察者的行为。
> 与 ch09 §9.5 的禁语第 10 条（「Saying, "Remember, we're not testing you," **more than three times**」）配套：**说了没兑现，重复三遍就成了反效果。**

> **原文（p199）**：
> 「do not be surprised if at the first sign of difficulty, you hear the participant mutter that familiar refrain, "**Oh, I'm an idiot. That wasn't the program's fault**," or "I just need more time to learn how to use it."」

> **★★★ 判据：被试自责 = 你的"不是在测你"没兑现**
> 这是现场可用的即时诊断信号。正确回应不是重复那句话，而是**把责任归给产品**（见 ch09 §9.4）。

### 7.3.6 脚本里绝对不能出现的三句话

> **原文（p198）**：
> 「**Avoid any reference whatsoever to your expectations of their behavior or performance.** Remain absolutely neutral in terms of their expected performance.」

> **原文（p198）**：
> 「For example, **do not say any of the following** in order to make participants less nervous:」
> - 「"**Most people find this extremely easy.**"」
> - 「"**We brought you in for an extremely simple test.**"」
> - 「"**I'm sure you'll have no difficulty with this product, so don't be nervous.**"」

> **原文（p198–p199）**：
> 「While well intentioned, these are **exactly the wrong things to say**. By making those references, **you have essentially put the participants on the defensive** if things do get difficult. At the slightest hint of adversity, they may begin to **hurry and try harder in order to fulfill your expectations**.」

> **★★★★ 判据：为缓解紧张而说"这很简单"，反而制造了压力**
> 机理（原文）：一旦真的难了，被试的推理是"简单 + 我做不出来 = 我笨"（此句为我的译述，非原文）。**所以宁可不说难度，也不要说低难度。**

### 7.3.7 两份完整脚本（原文图 8-2 / 8-3）

**图 8-2：基础测试的开场脚本（p194，全文照录）**

> **原文（p194）**：
> 「"Thank you for agreeing to take part in our research study. My name is Dana, and this is Russell. May I have your signed consent form, please? Thanks. [If they haven't brought a signed one with them, give one to them now and have them review and sign.]
> "During the rest of the session, I'll be working from a script to ensure that my instructions to everyone who participates in the study are the same.
> "I'm here to learn about how voters use a new web site.
> "During the session, I will ask you to use the web site to do a variety of things and will observe you while you do them. As you do these things, please try to do whatever you would normally do.
> "Please try to think out loud while you're working. Just tell me whatever is going through your mind. Please know that we're not testing you, and there is no such thing as a wrong answer. Your doing this helps us understand what works or doesn't work about the site.
> "By the way, I'm an independent researcher who had nothing to do with the design of the site you're about to try out. So please be honest in your feedback—I need to know exactly what you think, not what you think I want to hear. Russell is here from the secretary of state's office to learn and take notes.
> "The whole session will take about 30 minutes.
> "Do you have any questions before we begin? [Answer any questions.]"」

> **★★★ 判据：一句"我与设计无关"胜过十句"不是在测你"**
> 注意这份脚本的结构：**先给身份中立性**（independent researcher），再给"不是在测你"。
> 顺序不能反：**可信度来自身份，不来自承诺。**

**图 8-3：基准比对测试的开场脚本（p195，要点照录）**

> **原文（p195）**：
> 「In this session, we'll have you do typical tasks, to learn how these programs work for people like you. Please keep in mind that we're not testing you—it's you who are helping us evaluate the software.」

> **原文（p195）**：
> 「When you have completed the task, say aloud, "I'm done." Place your hands in your lap to signal that you're finished and waiting for the next task. [Moderator: Make sure the camera is at a wide angle on the user so you can see this behavior.]」

> **原文（p195）**：
> 「Start working on the task only once I have said, "Please begin." I'll start recording time as soon as you touch the keyboard or the mouse—so don't touch either one until you're ready to start the task.」

> **★★ 判据：计时类测试必须把"开始/结束信号"写进脚本**
> 因为计时精度取决于**边界的可判定性**，不取决于秒表。原文给了两个信号：「"I'm done"」+「hands in your lap」（后者是为了让摄像机能看到）。

## 7.4 现场主持：四类互动

### 7.4.1 何时干预（原文 §9.x 口径）

> **原文（p211）**：
> 「Whereas probing is the act of soliciting information from the participants and is often an integral part of the test design, **assisting the participants to complete a task is invasive and should only be done when absolutely necessary.**」

> **原文（p211）**：
> 「Let's be very clear about this. **As soon as you assist, you are affecting the test results in a major way.** If you are tracking the number of tasks performed correctly, **you need to differentiate between those that required assistance and those that did not. Never lump both of those categories together.**」

> **原文（p211）**：
> 「If you can, **wait until the participant has gone beyond the time benchmark for the current task** before providing assistance or offering a hint. At that point, **you have already scored the task as "unsuccessful" anyway**, and your assistance can no longer affect that compilation.」

五个"该帮"的时机（原文 p211–p212）：
1. 「When a participant is **very lost or very confused**.」
2. 「When performing a required task makes a participant **feel uncomfortable**.」
3. 「When a participant is **exceptionally frustrated and may give up**.」
4. 「When the product is in a **"before-final" state** and you need to provide missing information」
5. 「When a **bug** occurs or a participant's actions cause a malfunction that requires repairs.」

> **★★★★ 判据：帮忙的最佳时机是"该项已判定失败之后"**
> 这是本条最实用的技巧：**越过基准时间再帮，就不影响计分**。既救了被试的情绪，又保住了数据。

> **原文（p211）**：
> 「**NEVER, EVER blame the participants**, even indirectly, for a problem. The fastest way to lose and/or bias a participant is to blame the participant for problems during a test. Just as the "customer is always right," so is the adage, "**mistakes are always the fault of the product**."」

> **原文（p212）**：
> 「**Gradually provide more revealing hints** to get the participants past an obstacle, rather than revealing everything all at once. ... Providing hints lets you ascertain **the minimum amount of information required for error recovery**」

**逐级提示的四档（原文 p305，原文称此法为 "graduate the level of detail"）**：

> **原文（p305）**：
> 「**A slight variation of this technique is to graduate the level of detail you give in the answers to questions.** This way, you can quantify the number and level of moderator interventions when participants need assistance. For example, you might just say "**try again**," or "**try another way**," if the participant simply needs a nudge to persist ... Next you might give a **generic indication** of the right approach, for example, "**you have been in the right place**," or "**try a different area of the screen**." Finally, you may have to give **specific instructions** if a task must be completed to be able to continue with the session.」

> **★★★ 判据：提示要分档并计数——"帮了几次、帮到哪一档"是可用性的量化指标**
> 四档：① 重新鼓励（try again）② 方向提示（right place / different area）③ 一般方法 ④ 具体步骤。
> 原文价值：**"需要说到第几档才通"直接反映缺陷深度**（与 ch08 严重度挂钩）。

### 7.4.2 什么时候不能打断

> **原文（p243）**：
> 「**Pay attention to where participants become quiet.** This can indicate that they are concentrating on solving some problem. **Rather than nudging someone to tell you what she is thinking, it may be better to note the incident and ask about it later.**」

> **原文（p244）**：
> 「**Practice meaningful silences and patiently waiting.** It can be good in some situations to observe a behavior or ask a question and then wait. **Count to 20 or 30 before asking for clarification.**」

> **原文（p210）**：
> 「If the participant appears to you to have stopped, is stuck, or you feel he or she may be finished, **count silently to 20 or 30 before you intervene.** At that point, you might say, "**Tell me what you're thinking**," or "**How close do you feel you are to completing the task?**"」

> **原文（p210–p211）**：
> 「If you jump in too soon, because you notice the participant is finished, **you are confirming that he or she has performed correctly and undercutting that moment of indecision.**」

> **★★★★ 判据：被试"停了"不等于"做完了"——那几秒的不确定本身是数据**
> 原文点明的机制：过早确认 = 替被试回答了"我做对了吗"。
> **落地**：把"20–30 秒"设成硬规则，而不是凭感觉。

> **原文（p210）**：
> 「**Be especially careful if you are sitting close to the participants**, because if they notice you making a mark on your data collection sheet, that can signal them that they have completed a task」

> **★★ 判据：记笔记这个动作本身就是提示**
> 对策（原文 p211）：「The best way to prevent this problem altogether is to **have the participants signal when they are finished**, as part of the test protocol.」

### 7.4.3 该问什么：中性问题的清单

> **原文（p246）**：
> 「**Ask neutral questions rather than "loaded" ones that imply an answer.** An example of a loaded question is, "**Most people find this feature easy to use. How about you?**" A more neutral phrasing would be, "**Is this feature easy or difficult to use?**" or "**What were you thinking when you used that feature?**"」

> **原文（p246）**：
> 「**Ask questions that do not imply right or wrong answers.** Focus on the participants' preferences and the value they place on features and functions. Some additional examples of neutral questions are:」
> - 「**What are you thinking right now?**」
> - 「**You seemed surprised/puzzled/frustrated, what happened?**」
> - 「**Exactly how did that differ from what you expected to happen?**」
> - 「**Would you expect that information to be provided?**」
> - 「**How close was that to what you expected?**」

> **原文（p245）**：
> 「**Focus on what the participants expected to happen.** When the participants have obviously done something different from what was expected or are lost or confused, ask them **what they expected to happen** in order to understand the root cause of the situation. **Do not feel the need to describe in any way what you expected to happen.**」

> **原文（p245）**：
> 「**Act as a mirror to reflect back what the participants are saying** and help them to express their thoughts in a useful way. **Do not say too much and do not volunteer information** unless it is an administrative issue or logistical point.」

> **原文（p245–p246，跨页）**：
> 「**Do not always ask direct questions.** ... Direct questioning of the type, "**How did you get all the way over to that screen?**" will tend to make the participants **extremely defensive** as if they were being grilled. A better approach is to ask, "**How's it going? How close do you feel you are to completing**」
> 「**the task?**" Alternatively, **take the blame yourself** by saying something like, "**I missed what just happened. Could you tell me?**"」

> **★★★★ 判据：把责任揽到自己身上，是被试卡住时最有效的问法**
> 「"I missed what just happened. Could you tell me?"」——三个作用：① 不暗示做错 ② 承认自己没看懂 ③ 要求对方解释而非评判。
> **对照 Barnum 的"问 what/how 不问 why"**（B p233）：两者方向一致（都不施压），但 Rubin 给的是**句式**，Barnum 给的是**疑问词**。可组合使用。

### 7.4.4 不该做的四件事

> **原文（p247）**：
> 「**Don't show surprise.** ... **reacting with incredulity may destroy that atmosphere** because it puts a participant on the defensive.」

> **原文（p247）**：
> 「**Don't problem solve.** Do not use the testing time to fix problems that are discovered. ... **participants are neither qualified as designers, nor are they aware of the constraints of the project.**」

> **原文（p247）**：
> 「**Handle one issue at a time.** ... Avoid venturing off into several issues at once or revealing information that is yet to be covered.」

> **原文（p247）**：
> 「**During the session, limit your interruptions to short discussions.** Save longer issues for the debriefing session.」

> **原文（p209）**：
> 「**Don't "Rescue" Participants When They Struggle**」……「By not letting the participants struggle, **you lose the opportunity to understand what happens when people get lost and how they recover.**」

> **★★★★ 判据："不要救"的理由不是冷酷，是"练习效应不可观测"**
> 被试在迷失后**怎么恢复**，是产品恢复力的唯一证据。救了他，这段数据就永远见不到。

> **原文（p209）**：
> 「there is **absolutely no replacement for a struggling participant to convince a skeptical developer** that there actually are problems with his or her beloved product.」

> **★ 判据：挣扎的被试本身就是劝服工具**
> 这解释了为什么"让开发者现场看"比"写报告"有效（对照 ch04 §4.2.3）。

### 7.4.5 出错之后

> **原文（p210）**：
> 「**If You Make a Mistake, Continue On**」……「Do not panic if you inadvertently reveal information or in some other way bias the session. **Just continue on as if nothing happened.** At worst, **you will invalidate only a small portion of the test.**」

> **原文（p210）**：
> 「**If you have conducted dry runs of your protocol and pilot tested with a user, the chances of something in your script revealing some bias is minimal.** The mistakes typically happen in the **un-scripted parts** of the test, such as when you ask follow-up questions」

> **★★ 判据：失误集中在脚本外的部分——所以脚本要覆盖得越广越好**
> 这给"脚本写多长"提供了判据：**凡是会被追问的环节，都该有脚本化的问法。**

## 7.5 不许对主持人说的话：原文的"Top Ten"

> **原文（p264）**：
> 「We have discussed what you should do or say during a test. **What you do not say is equally important.** With all due respect to David Letterman, here then is our "**Top Ten List**" of Things Not to Say to Participants:」

> **原文（p265–p266）**（原文从 10 倒数到 1，此处按原序照录）：
> 「10. Saying, "**Remember, we're not testing you,**" **more than three times**.」
> 「9. **Don't worry, the last participant couldn't do it, either.**」
> 「8. **No one's ever done that before.**」
> 「7. **HA! HA! HA!**」
> 「6. **That's impossible! I didn't know it could go in upside down!**」
> 「5. **Could we stop for awhile? Watching you struggle like this is making me tired.**」
> 「4. **I didn't really mean you could press any button.**」
> 「3. **Yes, it's very natural for observers to cry during a test.**」
> 「2. **Don't feel bad, many people take 15 or 16 tries.**」
> 「1. **Are you sure you've used computers before?**」

> **★★★★ 判据：这十条禁语的共同结构是"把被试的困难归因到被试身上"**
> 逐条看，只有第 4、7、3 带玩笑性质，其余七条都在做同一件事：
> - 第 1 条：质疑资格（最致命）
> - 第 2 条：暗示"需要很多次才正常"→ 反而暴露了产品的糟糕
> - 第 5 条：表达自己的疲惫（被试会更自责）
> - 第 6 条：表达惊讶（= 你做错了）
> - 第 8 条：「No one's ever done that before」→ 你与众不同（负面）
> - 第 9 条：拿别的被试比较 → 你很差
> - 第 10 条：重复"不是在测你"超过三次 → 反而强化怀疑
>
> **自检规则**：**凡是句子里出现"你/别人/我"，并且带评价的，都不能说。**

> **★★★ 判据：第 10 条的"三次"是可计数的上限**
> 原文罕见地给了数字阈值。**含义**：这句话说一次是铺垫，说三次是暗示——暗示被试你其实在测他。

> **★★ 判据：第 9 条看似安慰，实则泄题 + 贬低**
> 「the last participant couldn't do it, either」同时做了两件坏事：告诉被试"这确实难"（改变了努力程度）+ 暗示"你也属于做不出来的那类"。

**补充：Barnum 的等值清单（对照）**

Barnum 用"该说什么"的正向形式给了同一批规则（B p232–p233）：

> **原文（B p232）**：
> 「**you have to provide balanced praise, no matter what happens. Balanced praise means giving positive feedback to task failure as well as task success.**」

> **原文（B p232）**：
> 「I have seen inexperienced moderators praise only successful task completion and avoid saying anything when participants experience task failure. **This unbalanced praise just reinforces participants' frustration at "failing"**」

> **原文（B p233）**：
> 「**Good questions begin with "what" or "how" (not "why")**」

> **原文（B p233）**：
> 「Questions that begin with "**why**" tend to suggest that **the participant has done something wrong.** They tend to put the participant on the defensive. Instead of saying, "**Why did you choose that button?**" rephrase the question to something less confrontational, such as "**How did you decide to choose it?**"」

> **原文（B p233）**：
> 「**Good questions are balanced** ... **Unbalanced example** : "So, how difficult was that for you?"」
> 「**Balanced example** : "So, **how easy or difficult** was that for you?" Or avoid the use of the adjectives altogether by phrasing the question: "**So, what was it like for you to do that?**"」

> **原文（B p236）**：
> 「**Don't say, "What is your problem here?" but ask, "What is your goal?" or "What are you thinking you should do here?"**」

> **原文（B p236）**：
> 「**Don't put words in the participant's mouth or offer interpretations.** If the participant says, "I'm not sure what to do here," don't say, "So you are confused because the menu bar is unclear?" Instead, say, "**What do you see as your options?**"」（B p236）

> **★★★★ 判据（两书合并）：禁语的三条通用规则**
> 1. **不评价**——不说"简单/难/奇怪/别人也这样"（Rubin 十条 + Barnum balanced praise）
> 2. **不归因于被试**——问 what/how/goal，不问 why/problem（Barnum p233/p236）
> 3. **不加解释**——不替被试总结原因（Barnum B p236 原句："Don't put words in the participant's mouth or offer interpretations."）
>
> 三者的共同目标：**让被试的困难保持在"产品的问题"这一归因上**（Rubin p211：「mistakes are always the fault of the product」）。

## 7.6 伦理与知情同意

### 7.6.1 三份必须签的文件

> **原文（p191）**：
> 「The test materials reviewed in this chapter are as follows:」
> - 「Orientation script」
> - 「Background questionnaire」
> - 「Data collection instruments」
> - 「**Nondisclosure agreement and recording consent form**」
> - 「Pre-test questionnaire」
> - 「Task scenarios」
> - 「Post-test questionnaire」
> - 「Debriefing topics guide」

> **原文（B p170）**：
> 「The consent form needs to **stipulate the purpose of the study and the ways in which the recorded material will be shared and used**. It needs to **explain how the participant will be identified**. ... The form needs to make clear that **the participant is free to stop at any time and to deny consent at any time.**」

> **原文（B p171）**：
> 「Any time you are testing with **minors — participants under 18 years of age — you need to get consent from a parent or guardian.**」

> **原文（B p170）**：
> 「Some organizations have strict requirements for obtaining this consent. **Universities typically require approval for studies by an institutional review board (IRB).**」

> **原文（B p170）**：
> 「It is common practice to request that this consent be given **before testing begins**. Some practitioners suggest that **the participant be asked to confirm consent again after the study concludes.**」

> **★★★★ 判据：同意书必须写四件事**
> ① 研究目的 ② 录像的去向与用途 ③ 被试如何被标识（真名/代号） ④ **可随时退出、可随时撤回同意**
> 未成年被试额外需监护人签署。

**Barnum 的同意书样例（B p171 全文照录要点）**：

> **原文（B p171）**：
> 「I hereby give my permission to be videotaped as part of my participation in the」……「Usability test conducted」……「[date] at the Usability Center at Southern Polytechnic.
> **Only my first name may be reported** in association with the session results.
> I understand and consent to the use and release of the video recording to the Usability Center and to the client. I further understand that the video recording and any highlights extracted from it may be used for review by the client and by the Usability Center. Representative video excerpts may also be used within presentations to the client, at professional meetings, and as part of research.
> I give up any rights to the video recording and understand that the recording may be used for the purposes described in this release form without further permission.
> **I understand that if for any reason I do not want to continue I can leave at any time during this recording session. I can also deny consent at any time.**」
> （原文两处填空下划线已用 `……` 省略，文字部分逐字照录。）

> **★★★ 判据：同意书要写明"录像会被做成 highlights 放给客户看"**
> 这是最容易被含糊带过的一项。若同意书只写"用于研究"，而实际做了宣传片，就是超范围使用（对照 ch06 §6.9 的 Rubin 要求：「only for data analysis and internal reports」）。
> **两书的差异**：Barnum 的样例**允许**用于客户演示与专业会议；Rubin 明确限定「only for data analysis and internal reports」。**签约时必须选定一种，并让通报口径与实际用途一致。**

### 7.6.2 隐私与代号

> **原文（p189）**：
> 「**Don't use names to label recordings or files.** ... you should **never use names — not even just first names — in reports. Refer to the participants by some other identifier, such as U1, U2, etc. ... or P1, P2, and so on**」

> **原文（p189）**：
> 「**Absolutely do not share video or audio recordings of participants for any reason other than the reasons that participants agreed to.** ... store the recordings securely, either on media that can be locked away or digitally protected with passwords.」

> **★★ 判据：Barnum 的"first name"口径比 Rubin 宽**
> Barnum 样例：「**Only my first name may be reported**」；Rubin：「**never use names — not even just first names**」。
> **本技能的裁决**：采用 Rubin 的更严口径（**只用 P1/U1 代号**）。理由：中文场景下"名"常足以识别个体（尤其小样本、内部测试）。

### 7.6.3 伦理的专业规范指向

> **原文（p90）**：
> 「**You should become familiar with the codes of ethics of the Usability Professionals' Association and the Human Factors and Ergonomics Society.** The links for each are available on the web site that accompanies this book」

> **★★ 判据：伦理依据要引专业团体规范，不是"我觉得合适"**
> 原文明确指向两个协会的伦理守则。本技能不复制其条文（避免与源书冲突），使用时查原网站。

### 7.6.4 一种特殊的伦理困境：不能让被试真的受损

> **原文（p237）**：
> 「All kidding aside, **in reality, you would never place the participant in jeopardy during the test**, and you would explain that at the appropriate moment during the test if asked.」

> **原文（p211）**：
> 「For example, the test may require the participants to perform an action that they ordinarily cannot bring themselves to do, such as **deleting a file without having a backup system in place**. (We're serious. **Certain actions are so deeply ingrained that people refuse to do**」
> 「them even if they know it's a test.)」（此句跨 p249/p250 页界，故分行照录。）

> **★★★ 判据：涉及"不可逆操作"的测试必须做假环境**
> 原文给的做法（p246 括注）：「set up the test so that **files are never really deleted, and can easily be restored**」。
> 伦理与数据双赢：既不让被试真受损，也保住了"敢不敢删"这一行为的可观测性。

## 7.7 记录与观察

> **原文（p224）**：
> 「**Record Start Time, Observe the Participant, and Collect All Critical Data**」

> **原文（p112）**：
> 「A data gatherer takes notes or **classifies the expected critical activities and events of a test into coded categories**. For example, if the participant is expected to access four screens during a test, **each screen could be categorized with a different letter: A, B, C, or D.**」

> **原文（p112）**：
> 「**At the same time, the data gatherer could also note the time** in order to cross-reference with the video or audio recording」

> **原文（p113）**：
> 「**We recommend that the test moderator not be the one who tracks the timings** as it is very labor intensive and distracts one from capturing the **what and why** of the participant's activities.」

> **★★★★ 判据：主持人不要自己计时——计时会吃掉"为什么"**
> 原文的分工建议：**数据采集员 / 计时员 / 产品技术专家 / 观察者**四类角色（p112–p113）。
> 单独做测试时，取"代码化记录"（A/B/C/D + 时间戳）而非逐字记录，以保住观察注意力。

> **原文（p95）**：
> 「**Too Involved with the Act of Data Collection** ... **the act of collecting that information should not interfere with direct observation**... even if that means that every aspect of the participant's behavior is not written down.」

> **★★★ 判据：记录不全可以接受，观察断线不可以**
> 依据：录像可回看，注意力不可回看。
